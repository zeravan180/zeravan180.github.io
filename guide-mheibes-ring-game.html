💍 Mheibes: the traditional ring game of Ramadan nights

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

Mheibes, sometimes written muhaibis and known in Kurdish as kustîrk (the ring), is one of the best-loved traditional games of Iraq. It is especially popular during the evenings of Ramadan, after iftar, when neighbourhood teams meet and play late into the night.

The idea of the game

The game is played by two teams. One team hides a ring in the closed hand of one of its players, and the other team must find it. Everything depends on reading faces and body language: a smile, a nervous glance or a hand held too tightly can give the secret away. The hiding team tries to stay calm and to confuse the searchers.

How a round works

  • The players of the hiding team sit in a row with their fists closed. The ring is passed secretly to one of them.
  • The searcher from the other team studies the fists and starts opening them. An empty hand is dismissed with a word that means "empty" – in many places the call is bat.
  • If the searcher clears the empty hands and leaves the ring for last, or points straight at the hand holding the ring, the searching team wins the point and takes the ring.
  • If the searcher opens the hand with the ring by mistake, the hiding team scores and hides the ring again.

Local rules vary from town to town – the number of players, the calls that are used and the number of points needed to win – but the spirit of the game is the same everywhere.

More than a game

Mheibes is part of the social life of Ramadan. Teams from different neighbourhoods challenge each other, crowds gather to watch, and tea and sweets are passed around. Some cities have held large public tournaments, and the game has often appeared on television during the holy month. It brings people together and fills the long evenings with laughter.

Tips for searchers and hiders

  • Searchers: take your time, look at faces rather than hands, and watch who avoids your eyes.
  • Hiders: keep the same expression whether you have the ring or not, and hold every fist the same way.
  • Bluffing is part of the fun – a confident hider can make an empty hand look suspicious.
