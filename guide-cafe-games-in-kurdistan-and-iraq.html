☕ Café games in Kurdistan and Iraq

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

For generations, the traditional café – chaykhana or maqha – has been a meeting place for men in towns across Kurdistan and Iraq. Over glasses of strong, sweet tea, friends talk about the news, family and work, and above all, they play.

The sound of tawla

The most familiar sound of the café is the rattle of dice and the click of checkers on a wooden tawla board. Players move quickly, call out the numbers – often using Persian or Turkish names for the dice – and comment on every lucky roll. Spectators gather around close games and do not hesitate to give advice.

Dominoes on the table

Dominoes is just as popular. The tiles are shuffled noisily face down on the table, and four players often play in teams of two. A good domino player remembers which tiles have been played, and partners develop an understanding without speaking.

Cards: Konkan and more

Card games, especially Konkan, are played at cafés and at home. Two packs of cards, jokers, and long discussions about whether a player should have opened earlier are all part of the game.

Chess and dama

Chess has a long history in the region – Baghdad was a world centre of chess more than a thousand years ago – and serious players can still be found in many cafés. Dama, with its straight moves and compulsory captures, is common too, often played with bottle caps or simple wooden pieces.

The ring game in Ramadan

During Ramadan, cafés stay open late, and many neighbourhoods play mheibes, the ring game, in teams. It turns the evening into a lively community event.

From the café to the phone

Today, many young people play the same games on their phones, often with friends who live in other cities or abroad. The atmosphere is different, but the games – and the friendly arguments about them – are the same. AG Classic Games was made to keep these games close to people wherever they are, in Kurdish, Arabic and English.
