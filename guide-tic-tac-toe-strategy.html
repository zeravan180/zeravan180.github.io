❌ Tic-tac-toe strategy: never lose again

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

Tic-tac-toe (XO) is the simplest strategy game there is, but it is a great introduction to thinking ahead. Mathematicians call it a "solved game": if both players play perfectly, it always ends in a draw. That means you can learn to never lose.

Why the centre and the corners matter

There are eight possible lines of three on a 3×3 board: three rows, three columns and two diagonals. The centre square is part of four of them, each corner is part of three, and each edge square is part of only two. That is why the centre is the strongest first move, and the corners come next.

A simple order of priorities

On every move, check these in order:

  1. Win – if you have two in a line and the third square is empty, take it.
  2. Block – if your opponent has two in a line, block the third square.
  3. Fork – make a move that creates two winning threats at once; the opponent can only block one.
  4. Block a fork – if your opponent could create a fork next move, prevent it, ideally by making a threat of your own that forces them to respond.
  5. Centre – take the centre if it is free.
  6. Opposite corner – if the opponent is in a corner, take the opposite corner.
  7. Empty corner, then empty edge.

A player who follows this list never loses.

The classic trap

If you start in a corner and your opponent replies on an edge next to it, you can often win. Take the centre or the opposite corner, and you will soon be able to create a fork. When you are the second player and your opponent starts in a corner, always answer in the centre – an edge reply is the most common way beginners lose.

The moving version: three marks each

Because perfect tic-tac-toe is always a draw, many people play a version where each player has only three marks. Once all six are on the board, players take turns moving one of their own marks to any empty square. Very old games such as Three Men's Morris and Achi work in a similar way, though they usually allow moves only along lines to neighbouring points.

The moving version is much richer. Some tips:

  • The centre is even more powerful, because it touches every line through the middle.
  • Before moving a mark, check which line you are breaking – your opponent may have been blocked only by that mark.
  • Try to create a position where any of your moves keeps two threats alive.
