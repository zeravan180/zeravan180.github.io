📜 A short history of dominoes

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

Dominoes is played everywhere from Latin America to the cafés of Erbil, Duhok and Baghdad. Its history is less well known than that of chess, but it is just as interesting.

Origins in China

The earliest known domino-like tiles come from China. Chinese tiles were closely related to dice: each tile showed the result of throwing two dice, and the sets were used for many different games. Written references to such tiles appear in Chinese sources by the Song dynasty (around the twelfth and thirteenth centuries), though the exact origin is uncertain.

Arrival in Europe

Dominoes as we know them appeared in Italy in the early eighteenth century, especially in Venice and Naples, and spread to France, where the game became fashionable. It is not certain whether the European game came directly from China or was invented separately. European sets differ from Chinese ones: they include blank faces and tiles with each pair of numbers only once, giving the familiar 28-tile double-six set.

The name "domino" is thought to come from French. One popular explanation is that the black tiles with white spots resembled the hooded masks or cloaks called dominos that were worn at carnivals, but the origin of the word is not completely clear.

Around the world

From Europe, dominoes travelled with sailors, soldiers and traders. It became extremely popular in Latin America and the Caribbean, where four-player partnership games are taken very seriously, and it found a natural home in the coffee houses of the Middle East. In Iraq and Kurdistan, a domino table with the sound of tiles being shuffled on the wood is a familiar part of café life.

Many games, one set

A single set of dominoes can be used for many games. The most common family is the "block" and "draw" games, where players match tiles end to end. In "draw" games a player who cannot play takes tiles from the boneyard, while in "block" games they simply pass. Other games score points when the open ends add up to a multiple of five ("All Fives" or "Muggins"). Larger sets, such as double-nine and double-twelve, make it possible for more people to play.

Dominoes today

Today dominoes is played in clubs, tournaments and, more and more, on phones. The basic appeal has never changed: the rules are easy to learn, a round takes only a few minutes, and there is always a mix of luck and skill.
