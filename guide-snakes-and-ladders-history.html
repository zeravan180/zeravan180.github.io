🐍 Snakes and ladders: an ancient game of fate

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

Snakes and ladders is often the very first board game a child learns. It has no decisions at all – everything depends on the die – but its history is surprisingly deep.

Moksha Patam

The game comes from India, where it was known by names such as Moksha Patam or Gyan Chaupar. It was used to teach moral lessons: ladders stood for virtues such as generosity, faith and humility, which lift a person upward, while snakes stood for vices such as anger, greed and pride, which pull a person down. The final square represented moksha, liberation. The game showed that a person's journey is shaped both by their actions and by fate.

Arrival in Britain and beyond

The game was brought to Britain in the nineteenth century, during the colonial period, and became a popular children's game. The moral meaning gradually faded, and the board settled into the familiar 10×10 grid of 100 squares. In the United States a version called Chutes and Ladders, with slides instead of snakes, was published in the mid-twentieth century.

A game of pure chance

Because players have no choices, snakes and ladders is a perfect example of a game of chance. Mathematicians have studied it as a "Markov chain": the position after each roll depends only on the current square and the number rolled. Using this method, one can calculate the average number of rolls needed to finish a particular board, which for typical boards is a few dozen. The exact number depends on where the snakes and ladders are placed.

This also explains why the game feels so dramatic. A long ladder near the start can carry a player far ahead, while a snake near the top can send the leader almost back to the beginning. Anyone can win, at any moment.

Why children (and adults) love it

  • It is easy to learn – children can play as soon as they can count.
  • It teaches counting, taking turns and accepting both good and bad luck.
  • Rounds are short and full of surprises.
