⚫ Dama (Turkish draughts)

Draughts where pieces move straight, not diagonally.

About the game

Dama is the form of draughts played in Turkey, Greece, Kurdistan and across the Middle East. Unlike the diagonal draughts of Europe, pieces move straight forward and sideways on every square of an 8×8 board, which makes the game fast and full of long capture chains.

How to play

  • Each player has sixteen pieces on the second and third rows. White moves first.
  • A piece moves one square forward, left or right – never backwards and never diagonally.
  • To capture, jump over an enemy piece next to you onto the empty square behind it. Captured pieces are removed.
  • Capturing is compulsory, and if there are several ways you must choose the one that captures the most pieces. Captures can continue in a chain during the same turn.
  • A piece that reaches the far row becomes a king (dama). A king moves any number of squares in the four straight directions and can capture from a distance.
  • You win when your opponent has no pieces left or cannot move.

Tips

  • Keep your pieces in solid groups; lonely pieces are easy targets.
  • Because captures are compulsory, you can force your opponent into a bad capture with a well-placed sacrifice.
  • Crowning a king early is very strong – a king can control a whole row or column.
