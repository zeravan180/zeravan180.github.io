▦ Dots and Boxes

Draw lines between dots, close boxes and claim more of the board than your opponent.

About the game

Dots and Boxes is a pencil-and-paper game first described in France in the nineteenth century by the mathematician Édouard Lucas. It is easy to learn and surprisingly deep: mathematicians have written whole books about its strategy.

How to play

  • The board is a grid of dots. AG Classic Games uses a large board of 15 × 10 boxes.
  • Players take turns drawing one line between two neighbouring dots, across or down.
  • If your line completes the fourth side of a box, the box is yours and you must draw another line.
  • When every line has been drawn, the player with the most boxes wins.

Tips

  • Avoid drawing the third side of a box – your opponent will close it.
  • Near the end, count the long chains of boxes and think about who will have to open the first one.
  • When your opponent opens a long chain, you can take all but the last two boxes and hand those two back, so that your opponent must open the next chain. See our strategy guide.
