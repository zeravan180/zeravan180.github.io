🎲 Backgammon (tawla) strategy: the best opening moves

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

Backgammon – tawla in our region – is a race, but it is not only a race. Every roll offers several choices, and the player who chooses better wins far more often over a long session. The opening roll is the best place to start learning.

The key points

Each player has 24 points to travel. Your home board is your last six points (your 1 to 6 points). Two points are especially valuable early in the game:

  • Your 5-point – in the middle of your home board. Holding it blocks the opponent's back checkers and later helps you close your board.
  • Your 7-point (the "bar point") – just outside your home board. Together with your 6-point it starts building a wall, or prime, that the opponent cannot jump.

Making a point means placing two or more of your checkers on it. A single checker – a blot – can be hit and sent back to the start.

Recommended first moves

Expert players and computer analysis agree on most opening moves. These are the standard choices:

  • 3-1: 8/5 6/5 – makes your 5-point. One of the best rolls in the game.
  • 4-2: 8/4 6/4 – makes your 4-point.
  • 6-1: 13/7 8/7 – makes your bar point, starting a strong block.
  • 5-3: 8/3 6/3 – makes your 3-point.
  • 6-5: 24/13 – "lover's leap": runs one back checker all the way to safety.
  • 6-4: 24/14, or 8/2 6/2 (make the 2-point), or 24/18 13/9 – all are reasonable.
  • 6-3 and 6-2: one checker from the back moves out (24/18) and one comes down from the mid-point (13/10 or 13/11).
  • 5-4, 5-2, 4-3: bring two checkers down from the mid-point (for example 13/8 13/9 with 5-4), building material for new points.
  • 2-1, 4-1, 5-1: often 13/11 6/5 (or 13/9 6/5, 13/8 6/5), "slotting" the 5-point with a checker you hope to cover next turn; 24/23 instead of 6/5 is a safer alternative.

Doubles are wonderful in the opening. For example, 6-6 makes both bar points (24/18(2) 13/7(2)), 4-4 is often played 24/20(2) 13/9(2) to take the opponent's 5-point, and 3-3 or 1-1 build points in your home board. (In standard backgammon rules each player throws one die to decide who starts, and the starter plays those two numbers, so the very first move is never a double; in some local versions the starter rolls again.)

Five practical rules

  1. Make points, not blots – when you can, move two checkers together to the same point.
  2. Count the shots – before leaving a blot, count how many dice combinations could hit it. A checker six or fewer points away from an enemy checker can be hit directly; further away, only combinations can reach it.
  3. Do not stack – five or six checkers on one point are wasted; spread them to build more points.
  4. Escape your back checkers – the two checkers on the opponent's 1-point are the hardest to bring home. Move them up before the opponent builds a block.
  5. Know the race – add up the pips (the total distance your checkers must travel). If you are well ahead in the race, avoid contact and run home; if you are behind, keep checkers back and wait for a shot.

The doubling cube

In many backgammon games, players use a doubling cube to raise the stakes when they feel ahead. AG Classic Games uses fixed stakes, so you can focus on the moves themselves, but the same idea applies: when your position is clearly better, play to keep it; when it is clearly worse, look for chances to hit.

Practise

Play a few games against the computer and pay attention only to your opening rolls. After a while, the right move will feel natural – and good openings make the rest of the game much easier.
