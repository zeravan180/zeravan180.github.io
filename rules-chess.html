♟️ Chess

The classic game of strategy for two players on an 8×8 board.

A short history

Chess grew out of the Indian game chaturanga around the sixth century and travelled through Persia, where it was called shatranj, into the Arab world and then to Europe. The modern rules, with the powerful queen and the long-moving bishop, settled in the fifteenth century. Today chess is played by hundreds of millions of people and is taught in schools as a way to train patience and careful thinking.

How the pieces move

  • King – one square in any direction. The game is about protecting your own king and trapping the other one.
  • Queen – any number of squares in a straight line or diagonal. It is the strongest piece.
  • Rook – any number of squares straight, along a row or a column.
  • Bishop – any number of squares along a diagonal, so it always stays on one colour.
  • Knight – jumps in an L shape (two squares one way, one square to the side) and is the only piece that can jump over others.
  • Pawn – moves one square forward (two on its first move) and captures one square diagonally forward. A pawn that reaches the last row becomes a queen or another piece.

Special rules

  • Castling: the king moves two squares towards a rook and the rook jumps to the other side of the king. It is allowed only if neither piece has moved, the squares between them are empty, and the king is not in check and does not pass through an attacked square.
  • En passant: if a pawn moves two squares and lands next to an enemy pawn, that pawn may capture it as if it had moved only one square, but only on the very next move.
  • Check and checkmate: a king that is attacked is in check and must escape at once. If there is no way out, it is checkmate and the game is over.
  • Draws: stalemate (no legal move but not in check), not enough pieces left to checkmate, the same position three times, or fifty moves without a capture or pawn move.

Tips for beginners

  • Control the centre early with your pawns and pieces.
  • Develop your knights and bishops before moving the queen many times.
  • Castle early to keep your king safe.
  • Before every move, ask: what is my opponent threatening?
  • Trade pieces when you are ahead in material, and avoid trades when you are behind.
