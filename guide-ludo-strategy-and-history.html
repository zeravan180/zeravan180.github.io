🎯 Ludo: history and winning strategy

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

Ludo is one of the most popular family games in the world. Everyone knows the basic idea – roll the die and race four tokens home – but there is more skill in it than most people think.

From pachisi to ludo

Ludo is a simplified version of pachisi, a cross-and-circle race game from India that has been played for centuries. Pachisi was traditionally played with cowrie shells instead of dice, and Mughal emperors are said to have played it on giant boards laid out in palace courtyards, using people as pieces. In the late nineteenth century, a simplified version with a cube die was patented in England under the name Ludo, Latin for "I play". Similar games are known as Parcheesi in the United States and Mensch ärgere Dich nicht in Germany.

Strategy: what to think about on each roll

A roll in ludo often gives you several choices. Making the right one, again and again, is what separates good players from lucky ones.

  • Get tokens out early. A six is your ticket out of the base. Having two or three tokens on the board gives you more choices for every roll.
  • Capture when you can. Sending an opponent's token back to its base costs them many turns. A capture is usually the best move available.
  • Stay out of range. An enemy token one to six squares behind yours can capture it with a single roll. When possible, move your tokens out of that range or onto safe squares.
  • Use the safe squares. Starting squares and marked safe squares protect your tokens. Resting on them while waiting for a good roll is often smart.
  • Do not rush one token. Running a single token all the way home while the others wait in the base can leave you with no useful moves later.
  • Watch the home stretch. Once a token enters its coloured home column it can no longer be captured. Getting tokens there safely is more important than speed.

Thinking about probability

With one die, every number from 1 to 6 has the same chance: one in six. This tells you how dangerous a position is. A token that can be reached by two different opponents' tokens is in much more danger than a token that only one opponent can reach. And a token right in front of an enemy token (one square away) is no safer than one six squares away – each needs one specific number.
