🎲 Backgammon (Tawla)

A race around the board decided by dice, skill and timing.

About the game

Backgammon, known in much of the Middle East as tawla, is one of the oldest board games in the world; boards of similar race games over four thousand years old have been found in the region. Two players race their fifteen checkers around a board of twenty-four narrow triangles called points.

How to play

  • Each turn you roll two dice and move your checkers by the numbers shown. Each die is a separate move; doubles are played four times.
  • You may land on any point that is empty, holds your own checkers, or holds just one enemy checker.
  • Landing on a single enemy checker (a blot) hits it and sends it to the bar. It must re-enter from the start before its owner can move anything else.
  • A point with two or more of your checkers is safe – your opponent cannot land there.
  • When all your checkers are in your home board you start bearing them off. The first player to bear off all fifteen wins.

Tips

  • Building several safe points in a row (a prime) blocks your opponent's checkers.
  • Avoid leaving single checkers where your opponent can easily hit them.
  • Early in the game, use high rolls to bring your back checkers forward.
  • Count the race: if you are far ahead, stop taking risks and simply run home.
