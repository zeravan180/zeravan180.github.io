🔲 Dominoes strategy: counting tiles and controlling the ends

By the AG Classic Games team · Updated 9 November 2026

At first sight dominoes looks like a game of luck: you get seven tiles and play the ones that match. But in cafés across Kurdistan and Iraq, the same players win again and again. The reason is that dominoes rewards memory, counting and patience.

Know the set

A standard double-six set has 28 tiles. Each number from 0 (blank) to 6 appears on exactly eight half-tiles: seven tiles contain each number, and the double counts twice. For example, the sixes are 6-0, 6-1, 6-2, 6-3, 6-4, 6-5 and 6-6. This simple fact is the base of all domino strategy, because it lets you know how many tiles of a number are still unseen.

Count what has been played

Every time a tile is played, it gives you information. If five tiles with a 4 are already on the table and you hold one more, only one tile with a 4 can still be in your opponents' hands (or in the boneyard). Good players keep a rough count of each number. You do not need to remember everything – start by watching the numbers that are on the open ends.

Watch what opponents cannot play

When a player passes or draws, they are telling you something: they have no tile that matches either end. If an opponent passes when the ends are 3 and 5, you know they have no threes and no fives. Remember it. Later, you can try to leave those numbers on the ends to block them again.

Play your heavy tiles early

In most scoring systems, the tiles left in your hand at the end count against you (or for your opponent). Doubles and high tiles such as 6-6, 6-5 and 5-5 are expensive to be caught with. When you have a choice between two playable tiles, getting rid of a heavy one is usually wise, especially early in the round.

Be careful with doubles

A double can only connect to one number, so it is easier to get stuck with it. If you hold 2-2 and most of the twos have already been played, it may never find a place. Play doubles when the chance appears, unless keeping the number on the board helps you block an opponent.

Control the ends

The best position in dominoes is to control which numbers are on the ends of the line. If you hold several tiles of one number, try to keep that number open: you will always have a move, while opponents who lack it must pass. This is how a "block" happens – the game stops because nobody can play, and the hand with the lowest count wins.

Playing with a partner

In four-player dominoes, partners sit opposite each other and win or lose together. Partnership play adds a new layer:

  • Help your partner – if your partner played a number, they probably have more of it. Leaving that number open helps them.
  • Do not block your partner – before closing a number, think about whether your partner might need it.
  • Read the signals – the first tile a player chooses often shows their strongest number.
  • Let the stronger hand lead – if your partner is close to going out, support them instead of chasing your own points.

Practise in AG Classic Games

Try playing a few rounds against the computer while counting just one number – say, the sixes. Once that becomes easy, count two numbers. Within a short time you will find yourself predicting which tiles your opponents hold, and your results will improve noticeably.
